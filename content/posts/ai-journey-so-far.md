In the middle of 2022, I felt the need to switch the team or the company. My
[then employer](https://redhat.com) encouraged lateral moves. But since I was
so sure about changing, I was exploring outside as well. Since around July
2022, tech companies started laying off in large numbers. It caused me to
rethink. I gave as much importance to internal move as to external one.

In parallel timelines, I got both an internal offer to move to the team working
on [KubeVirt](https://kubevirt.io) as well as an external offer from a 
[SaaS-focussed database company](https://www.couchbase.com). The former offered
a measly 3% salary hike, while the latter offered more than 30% hike in fixed
salary plus a great (to me) amount of stocks that would vest every year. But,
according to balance sheet, that company was loss-making.

Before I could make up my mind, ChatGPT was announced on Nov 30, 2022. As if
the series of layoffs that started n July 2022 wasn't enough, the internet
was flooded with doomsday predictions for software engineers' job market. The
combination of incessant layoffs and AI predictions made me panic. I chose to
make a lateral move.

Don't get me wrong, the work on KubeVirt was amazing. I loved every bit of it
because no day was less than a challenge on the technical front. And I was
working in a team of super smart individuals who knew Linux Virtualization
extremely well. But the other role was a pure Go backend role with almost no
Kubernetes—something I had been craving for at that point in time. My reasoning
to the hiring team at Couchbase was consistent—I feared the aspect that the
company was not profitable on the books, and staying at Red Hat felt "safer" as
the sole breadwinner in the house. My friends who know the details, still make
fun of me for leaving that offer. :smile:

Four months later, in April 2023, Red Hat announced its first-ever round of
layoffs. I wasn't impacted, but it scared the hell out of me. I received a ping
from one of the members of the hiring team at Couchbase asking how I felt about
it. Obviously, I wasn't feeling good. Anyway, I eventually moved to
[SUSE](https://suse.com) in April 2024, most importantly for financial reasons.

How's all this relevant to AI journey? Maybe it's not. I'm just establishing
_context_! :wink:

### The Anxious Individual phase — late 2022 to early 2026

Since ChatGPT's launch till a few months back, I have stayed very anxious and
afraid of coding tools coming after my job. And in India, the labour laws
aren't how they are in the EU. Companies don't need to give you a heads-up
before reducing headcount. As numerous folks have shared their experience, you
just wake up one day to find out you are no longer employed by means of not
being able to log in to your company laptop. The oversimplification of this
thing is scary!

The way the leaders of companies building frontier models keep claiming about
how AI is going to make every job, and especially software development,
obsolete is nothing short of fear inducing and mental health f**kery
unputdownable in words. This caused me to stay at some distance from AI tools.
I would still use it for my job, but I was never curious about AI itself. About
any of its technicalities. The fear, anxiety, and stress kept increasing
because I mainly read the headlines. For someone who claims to love books and
reading, it was so wrong to just go by headlines that are meant to feed, cause,
and grow mostly the negative emotions.

Before the end of 2025, I made a lateral move within SUSE and joined a team
that's doing some foundational work at the union of Linux & Kubernetes. Imagine
working on Kubernetes Operators and Linux low-level things like initramfs,
booting process, ignition, etc. at the same time. The work is interesting;
every day is a challenge, I learn new stuff everyday, and there's usually no
precedent to follow. There's novelty in _how_ the end product should look,
feel, and work.

### The Absolutely Interested phase

I now use AI tools to brainstorm now and then, to make it write code or tests
(which I run & review first before opening a PR), and, most importantly, as an
overall research buddy so that I don't turn up in a meeting or PR review
sounding completely dumb/naive! :wink:

Of course, I see how fast these tools are progressing at doing my job faster
and, sometimes, better. Sure, I see how they make me a better engineer and
thinker sooner than I could have become without using them.

But by reading and listening to the conversations beyond just the headlines has
helped me cope and come out of the uncomfortable cocoon I was earlier in. Since
I am still surrounded by super smart individuals at work, I silently follow the
conversations about their experiences with AI and their thoughts on
fearmongering headlines. Some recent podcast episodes, like 
[this one from Cal Newport](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aHxwZEXHwps) and
[this one from Adam Gordon Bell](https://corecursive.com/the-pre-training-wall-and-the-treadmill-after-it/),
have filled me with hope.

I am starting to feel interested in conversations about technicalities of AI
like tokens, base models, distillation, quantization, etc., and learn more about
these topics. Heck, I even succeeded in running a couple of models—Qwen3 8B and
Gemma4 4B—locally over the past weekend. While it's probably a "meh" thing in
2026, it made me feel surprisingly good about doing and using it.

### That's it!

In spite of the gradual change of my mental state, I'm still consciously aware
that job safety is not guaranteed. The world of software, which felt like a
safe haven when I entered the job market, is now anything but safe! 

But I'm happy about the return of curiosity and energy that I seemed to have
forgotten the feeling of in mind & body, and excited about the evolution of
the larger AI journey which we are only in the beginning of! :smile:
